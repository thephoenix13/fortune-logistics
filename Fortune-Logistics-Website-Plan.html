01
Objective & Overview
Fortune Logistics needs a B2B corporate website that generates qualified freight enquiries and communicates reliability to logistics buyers — procurement heads, supply chain managers, and fleet managers at mid-to-large companies across India.
The Problem
  • No digital presence to back up sales pitches
  • Buyers can't verify capabilities before calling
  • No channel for inbound RFQs from new clients
  • Competitors with websites win at first impression
The Website Does
  • Builds credibility before the first call
  • Shows fleet, coverage, and services in detail
  • Captures RFQs from buyers actively searching
  • Surfaces on Google for freight-related searches
Primary goal: Every page should answer one question — "Can Fortune Logistics handle my shipment?" — and end with a clear path to contact or request a quote.

03
Page-by-page Breakdown
What goes on each page and what it is designed to accomplish.
P1
Homepage
/
The first impression for every visitor. Must communicate who Fortune Logistics is, what they carry, where they operate, and give a clear next step — all above the fold on mobile.
Hero — Headline, subhead, CTA button (Get a Quote), background image of fleet
Stats Bar — 50+ cities · 120+ fleet · 98% on-time · 500+ clients (animated counters)
Services Overview — 4-card grid: FTL · PTL · ODC · Packaging, each linking to service sub-page
Industries Strip — Horizontal icon+name strip: Automotive, FMCG, Pharma, Industrial
Network Teaser — India map thumbnail with "50+ cities" overlay + "See Network" link
Why Fortune — 3 differentiators: GPS tracking, on-time commitment, dedicated account manager
Testimonials — 2–3 client quotes with company name (if client provides)
CTA Banner — Full-width banner: "Ready to move your freight?" + quote button
Hero image: Fortune's own truck photos strongly preferred over stock. Single most impactful piece of photography for the site.
P2
About Us
/about
Humanizes the company. Buyers choosing a logistics partner want to know who they're trusting with their cargo. This page turns Fortune from a vendor into a company with a story and values.
Origin Story — Who founded it, why, and what was the gap they saw
Mission & Values — 3–4 values with short descriptions (reliability, transparency, etc.)
Timeline / Milestones — Year-by-year growth (2019 founded → cities → fleet → 2026)
Leadership — Founder / key team with photo, name, short bio (1–2 people)
Certifications — Any GST, ISO, or government freight permits
Office / Location — Registered office address, embedded Google Map
P3
Services (Hub + 4 Sub-pages)
/services + /services/ftl /ptl /odc /packaging
The hub page gives a quick overview. Each sub-page goes deep into one service — what it is, who it's for, how it works, and why Fortune does it well. Sub-pages are critical for SEO: "FTL transport Pune" and similar searches land here.
Hub: Service Grid — 4 cards, each with icon, name, 1-line description, Read More link
Hub: CTA — "Not sure which service you need? Talk to us."
FTL Sub-page — What is FTL, capacity (tons/volume), vehicle types, loading process, pricing model, when to use FTL vs PTL
PTL Sub-page — Consolidated freight, how it works, minimum weight, transit times, tracking
ODC Sub-page — Oversized & project cargo, special permits, escort vehicles, equipment types handled
Packaging Sub-page — Materials offered, transit protection, customization, photos of packaged goods
Each sub-page ends with an inline quote request form so visitors don't have to navigate away to enquire.
P4
Fleet & Capabilities
/fleet
Buyers evaluating logistics partners want to see the actual vehicles — type, capacity, age, and technology. This is a trust-builder. Most local logistics companies don't publish this — Fortune doing so is a differentiator.
Fleet Grid — Cards for each vehicle type: name, capacity (tons), load volume (CBM), photo
Vehicle Types — Mini truck · 3-ton · 7-ton · 10-ton · 14-ton · 20-ton trailer (based on actual fleet)
Capabilities Table — Filterable table: vehicle × payload × dimensions × best for
Technology — GPS real-time tracking, digital POD, SIM-based tracking if applicable
Fleet Stats — Total owned vehicles, average fleet age, maintenance schedule
Special Equipment — Flatbed for ODC, open body, closed body, refrigerated (if any)
Needs actual vehicle photos from Fortune. Even basic phone photos work better than stock here — authenticity matters.
P5
Industries Served (Hub + 4 Sub-pages)
/industries + /automotive /fmcg /pharma /industrial
Industry-specific pages let a pharma company see that Fortune understands their constraints (temperature, documentation) without reading through generic logistics content. These pages also rank for niche SEO terms like "pharma logistics Pune."
Automotive — Auto parts, CKD/SKD, JIT delivery, OEM supply chain context
FMCG — High-volume distribution, cold chain awareness, seasonal surge handling
Pharmaceuticals — Temperature compliance, documentation, GDP-aware handling
Industrial / Mfg — Machinery, heavy goods, ODC-linked, factory-to-factory
Per-page structure — Challenge → Fortune's approach → Key services used → Client type served
Cross-links — Each industry page links to relevant service sub-pages
P6
Our Network
/network
Answers the buyer's first question: "Do you cover my lane?" An interactive map showing all 50+ cities served, major route corridors, and hub locations turns this into the most engaging page on the site.
Interactive India Map — Dot markers for all served cities, click to see city name + lane options
Route Corridors — Line overlays for key lanes (Pune–Mumbai, Pune–Delhi, etc.)
City List — Searchable A–Z list of all cities served (fallback if map fails)
Hub Locations — Fortune's depot/hub cities highlighted differently
Coverage Stats — States covered, pin-codes served, transit day estimates by zone
"Don't see your city?" — Inline CTA: "We may still cover it. Ask us." → contact form
Map built with SVG or Leaflet.js. City data populated from a JSON list Fortune provides — easy to update without code changes.
P7
Why Fortune
/why-fortune
The sales page without being a sales page. Converts a visitor who is comparing multiple logistics companies — gives them specific, verifiable reasons to pick Fortune over generic providers.
Key Differentiators — 5–6 cards with icon, heading, 2-line description (GPS tracking, dedicated PoC, real-time updates, etc.)
On-time Commitment — 98% on-time rate with context: how it's measured, what happens if late
Transparency — Live tracking, digital POD, WhatsApp updates
Client Testimonials — 3–5 real quotes with name, company, cargo type
Comparison Table — Fortune vs. unorganized broker: GPS, real POD, fixed rates, accountability
Certifications / Memberships — Any trade body memberships, GST compliance badge
P8
Contact & Request a Quote
/contact
The conversion page. Every page on the site funnels here. The form should be short enough to complete in 90 seconds and specific enough to give Fortune the information they need to respond with a real quote.
RFQ Form Fields — Name · Company · Phone · Email · Origin city · Destination city · Service type · Cargo description · Approx weight · Target date
Form Submission — Email notification to Fortune's inbox + auto-reply to enquirer
WhatsApp Button — Prominent "Chat on WhatsApp" with pre-filled opening message
Direct Call — Phone number large and tappable on mobile
Office Info — Address with Google Maps embed
Business Hours — When to expect a response
After submission, redirect to /contact/thank-you with a message like "We'll get back within 4 business hours." Set the response expectation explicitly.

SEO & Performance Strategy
The goal is to rank for freight and logistics searches in Fortune's key markets within 3–6 months of launch.
Target Keywords
  • Truck transport Pune
  • FTL transport Maharashtra
  • PTL logistics Pune to Mumbai
  • ODC cargo transport India
  • Logistics company Pune
  • Freight service Pune
  • Automotive logistics Pune
  • Pharma transport Pune
  • Full truck load hire Maharashtra
  • Freight company India B2B
What We Do for SEO
  • Unique meta title + description per page
  • H1/H2 with keyword placement
  • LocalBusiness schema markup (JSON-LD)
  • Google Search Console setup + sitemap submit
  • Google Business Profile setup / claim
  • City-specific content on network page
  • Image alt text for all photos
  • Internal linking between related pages
  • Page speed: target 90+ on Lighthouse
  • Mobile-first indexing compliant
Realistic expectation: A new domain takes 3–6 months to build authority. The site will be indexed within days of launch. Ranking on page 1 for competitive terms takes consistent content and links — the foundation we build at launch makes that possible. Local and long-tail terms ("FTL Pune to Nagpur") can rank faster — sometimes within weeks.
